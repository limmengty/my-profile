Every developer gets stuck. The difference between junior and senior engineers isn't that seniors never get stuck — it's that they have better systems for getting unstuck.

> **TL;DR:** Time-box your attempts to 25 minutes, rubber duck before asking for help, and walk away when you're looping. The real skill is recognizing when to change approach.

## The Stuck Spiral

Getting stuck triggers a predictable spiral: you try the same approach repeatedly, get frustrated, lose confidence, and eventually stop making progress entirely. The longer you stay in the spiral, the harder it is to break out.

> The spiral isn't a skill problem. It's a recognition problem — you don't notice you're in it until you've already wasted an hour.

## Time-Box Your Attempts

Give yourself a hard limit — **25 minutes on one approach**. If you haven't made progress, stop and do something different. This isn't giving up; it's recognizing that more time on a broken approach rarely fixes it.

## Rubber Duck First

Before asking anyone, explain the problem out loud to an inanimate object. This sounds ridiculous but works. The act of articulating the problem forces you to examine your assumptions. Half the time, you'll find the bug mid-sentence.

## Ask Better Questions

When you do ask for help, don't ask _"why doesn't this work?"_

Ask: **"I expected X, I got Y, here's what I've tried."**

This framing respects the other person's time and often reveals the answer before they respond.

## Walk Away

Physical movement breaks mental loops. **A 10-minute walk is not wasted time** — it's often the most productive 10 minutes of your day. Your subconscious keeps working on the problem while your conscious mind rests.

## The Meta-Skill

> The real skill isn't solving any particular problem. It's recognizing when your current approach isn't working and having the discipline to change it. That's what separates engineers who grow from engineers who plateau.

**Quick reference:**

- ⏱ 25 minutes on one approach, then switch
- 🦆 Rubber duck before asking for help
- 💬 "I expected X, I got Y" — not "why doesn't this work?"
- 🚶 10-minute walk when you're looping
